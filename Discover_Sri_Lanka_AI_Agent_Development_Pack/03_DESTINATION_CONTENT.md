# Destination Content Library — Ready for Website Editorial Data

**Writing standard:** concise guest-facing English, 1–3 short paragraphs per screen section. Distinguish **documented history**, **living religion**, **local tradition**, and **practical guidance**. Each entry includes editorial starter copy, not a certification of future opening times or access. For more source details, see `13_SOURCES_AND_RESEARCH.md`.

## Place metadata conventions
- **P0:** core itinerary stop; **P1:** recommended if practical; **P2:** only when time allows.
- **Narrative tags:** `HERITAGE`, `BUDDHISM`, `HINDUISM`, `RAMAYANA_TRADITION`, `NATURE`, `CULTURE`, `COAST`.
- **Site practical info:** mark as `Confirm before visit` until up-to-date local sources or operator confirm it.
- **Image requirement:** photo of the actual site, legally licensed, with image credit and meaningful alt text.
- **Navigation:** explicit Google Maps/place query or validated coordinates maintained in a central data file; never invent GPS coordinates.

## 02. Sigiriya Rock Fortress

**Slug:** `sigiriya-rock-fortress` | **Day:** 22 | **Priority:** P0 | **Tags:** HERITAGE

**Tagline:** An ancient royal city above the forest.

**Guest summary:** Sigiriya is one of Sri Lanka's most famous archaeological sites, where an immense rock rises above historic gardens and a remarkable royal landscape. UNESCO associates the site's great fifth-century royal capital with **King Kassapa I (477–495 CE)**. The site offers a powerful view of Sri Lanka's architectural and cultural history.

**Look for:** landscape and water gardens, monumental rock, traces of the palace complex, surviving painted areas where viewing is permitted, panoramic viewpoint (optional climb).

**Fun fact:** Sigiriya is a UNESCO World Heritage property, inscribed in 1982.

**Practical:** The summit involves substantial climbing and exposure to heat; give guests a non-summit alternative. Take water, shade protection and suitable footwear. Confirm ticket price and rules from CCF before visit. Do not promise accessibility to the summit.

**Ravana link (accurate phrasing):** Some travel stories connect Sigiriya with Ravana, but the site's established historical interpretation concerns King Kassapa. Present any Ravana association only as an unverified local tradition, never as a proven fact.

**Host opening line:** "The rock is unforgettable, but the real wonder is how the whole landscape was planned around it."

**Source:** https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/202/ ; https://ccf.gov.lk/vsl/tickets

## 03. Dambulla Cave Temple

**Slug:** `dambulla-cave-temple` | **Day:** 22 | **Priority:** P0 if feasible | **Tags:** HERITAGE, BUDDHISM

**Tagline:** Ancient painted caves that remain places of worship.

**Guest summary:** Dambulla's cave temple complex is a major Buddhist heritage site in Sri Lanka. Its richly decorated shrines contain Buddha images and wall paintings, and the caves continue to be important to worshippers. UNESCO describes five principal cave sanctuaries.

**Look for:** ceiling paintings, rows of images, the contrast between rocky cave spaces and devotional art.

**Practical:** Dress respectfully, remove shoes where instructed, and follow photography restrictions. Be careful on hot or slippery steps. Verify opening hours and access locally; don't assume Sigiriya + Dambulla is always comfortable on a day beginning at the airport.

**Host opening line:** "Unlike an abandoned monument, this is a living religious space with centuries of devotion."

**Source:** https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/561/ ; https://tourism.cp.gov.lk/en/dambulla-royal-cave-temple

**Guest summary:** This Hindu temple near Nuwara Eliya is especially significant to visitors following the Ramayana traditions of Sri Lanka. Local belief links this area with Sita's time in Lanka and includes stories surrounding a nearby stream and markings associated with Hanuman. ## 14. Ravana Cave (Rawana Cave), Ella

**Slug:** `ravana-cave` | **Day:** 25 (backup: early 26 only if feasible) | **Priority:** P1-high for interest, conditional on safe access | **Tags:** RAMAYANA_TRADITION, NATURE

**Tagline:** A hillside cave surrounded by one of the island's great legends.

**Guest summary:** Ravana Cave near Ella is associated in local Ramayana tradition with accounts of Ravana and Sita. It is a compelling place to consider how mythology becomes part of a real landscape. The cave's physical presence is real; its identification with epic episodes is a matter of tradition, not established archaeological fact.

**Look for:** the forested hillside, cave entrance, views and the feeling of the natural setting. Respect other visitors and any religious practices.

Avoid in rain, low light or if guests are uncomfortable with strenuous climbing. Never assume Ravana Cave is accessed from the waterfall's roadside viewing area by an easy stroll; these are separate visitor experiences.

## Editorial QA rules for every record

1. Don't place exact opening times or fees in prose unless a source was checked very recently and the record carries a verification date.
2. Don't identify all "Ravana" tourist sites as evidence that a historical King Ravana lived there.
3. Never promise a waterfall swim, unsafe climb, night cave access, or railway-track photo.
4. If no photo license is available, show a tasteful illustrated placeholder with text *Image coming soon*, not a stolen photograph.
5. Add pronunciation hints only where verified; don't mock or reduce local religious names.
6. Display `Tradition` labels next to legend claims, not buried behind an info icon.
7. On a detailed page, start with the 70–100-word quick summary and progressively disclose longer content.
