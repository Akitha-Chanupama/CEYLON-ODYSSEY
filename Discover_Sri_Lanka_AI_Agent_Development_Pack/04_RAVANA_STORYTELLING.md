# Ravana & Ramayana Storytelling Framework

## Mission

Because the two guests particularly enjoy stories about **Ravana (Rawana)**, make the Ramayana connections a first-class narrative thread across the itinerary. The site should feel like a carefully curated **Ravana Trail** within the larger Sri Lankan journey, not like random tourist facts added to cards.

**Non-negotiable accuracy rule:** The **Ramayana is a revered epic with diverse religious, literary and local traditions**. Treat claims that particular present-day Sri Lankan sites witnessed exact epic events as **beliefs or associations**, not verified archaeological events. Do not imply all Hindus, Buddhists or Sri Lankans interpret the epic identically.

## Editorial labels

- **`TRADITION` — "According to local Ramayana tradition…"** Epic connections, sacred narratives, folk beliefs, alternate tellings.
- **`HERITAGE` — "Archaeology/history tells us…"** UNESCO findings, well-documented construction periods, known historical figures.
- **`OBSERVE` — "What you can see today…"** Caves, waterfalls, temples, gardens, living worship, landscape.
- **`UNCERTAIN` — "Accounts vary…"** Disputed identifications, uncertain place names, changing access details.

Place a small `Tradition` label **next to** claims rather than on the whole page. Don't frame tradition dismissively; use respectful phrasing.

## Guest-facing narrative: a short introduction

> Ravana is one of the most memorable figures in the Ramayana, an epic told and retold across South Asia. Different communities and retellings portray him in different ways. In Sri Lanka, a number of temples, natural landmarks and place names are linked to these stories. During this trip we'll visit real places where the traditions are remembered — while keeping the fascinating stories separate from what archaeology can prove.

## Narrative cards ready to adapt

### Hanuman and the hills of Ramboda
**Tradition:** In accounts associated with the Ramayana, Hanuman travels in search of Sita. The Ramboda region is one of the landscapes connected with that search in Sri Lankan pilgrimage traditions. At the temple, guests can observe a living devotional tradition rather than a museum reconstruction.

### Sita and Seetha Eliya
**Tradition:** Local belief links Seetha Eliya with Sita's stay in Lanka. The current kovil is an important place for devotees, and stories about the nearby stream and marks in stone form part of that religious landscape. These links are spiritually meaningful, but not independently documented epic-era events.

### The Ashoka Vatika connection
**Tradition:** Hakgala's surrounding landscape is often associated with the garden in which Sita was held in the epic. Explain the connection as **tradition**. The modern botanical garden has its own history and natural attractions.

### Inside the Ravana Cave story
**Tradition:** Some local accounts identify a hillside cave near Ella with the places connected to Ravana and Sita. The cave is a real geological place; the identification is traditional. Do not repeat unverified claims of secret tunnels, exact ancient engineering, or proven underground networks.

### Ravana Falls
**Tradition and nature:** A powerful waterfall near Ella carries Ravana's name. The legend adds a layer of narrative to the landscape, but the waterfall's beauty can be appreciated without treating the story as archaeological evidence.

### Rumassala and the mountain of healing herbs
**Tradition:** In the Ramayana, Hanuman's search for a healing herb is a famous episode. Sri Lankan traditions associate Rumassala with a fragment of the healing mountain. Whether or not the story is read literally, the coastal hill is a memorable final chapter for this journey.

## Guide storytelling method — 60-second format

For every thematic stop:
1. **10 seconds: Place** — "This is [site], near [town]."
2. **20 seconds: Story** — "According to local tradition…"
3. **15 seconds: What is visible** — "Today you can see…"
4. **10 seconds: Distinction** — "It's a tradition; the physical/historical site has its own story too."
5. **5 seconds: Invitation** — "Would you like to see [view/detail]?"

Store this script in a **host-only short-form note** plus optional longer source notes.

## A 'Learn more' prompt template for editorial AI

```text
Write a 180–250-word destination story for international visitors about [LOCATION].

three quick facts, and a host's natural 60-second script.
```

## Editorial prohibitions

- No fake "Ravana's scientifically proven flying machine" claims.
- No claim that a site contains confirmed Ravana-era tunnels based on tourist folklore.
- No conflating the Buddhist heritage of Sigiriya or Dambulla with Ravana narratives.
- No claims that modern temple complexes date from the epic's alleged events without evidence.
- No framing Ravana as uniformly good or bad across all cultural retellings.
- No disrespectful illustrations of living religious figures or rituals.

## On-page narrative UI

On a Ravana-related page, use a compact editorial segment:

`✦ PART OF THE RAVANA TRAIL` → site-specific story card → `Tradition` label → short paragraph → `What you'll see today` → next related stop → source link.

Offer a visual chapter progression such as:

**Ramboda → Seetha Eliya → Ella Cave → Ravana Falls → Rumassala**

Do not imply users must complete a physical hike, cave visit or unsafe access to "complete" the trail; meaningful participation can be reading/viewing only.
