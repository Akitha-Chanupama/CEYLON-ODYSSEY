## Ravana Trail hero text

**Subheading:** From sacred highland temples to caves, waterfalls and coastal hills, follow the places where the Ramayana is remembered in Sri Lanka.

## Sample host 60-second scripts

### 1. Sigiriya

"Welcome to Sigiriya, one of the most remarkable heritage sites in Sri Lanka. This enormous rock rises above the remains of an ancient royal landscape. UNESCO links the royal city to King Kassapa in the fifth century, and the surviving gardens and architecture still show impressive planning. Some popular legends connect this rock with Ravana, but the archaeological story we know is about Kassapa. Whether we climb or enjoy the views from below, it's a wonderful introduction to Sri Lanka's long history."

### 2. Dambulla

"These caves aren't simply historical rooms; they belong to a Buddhist place of worship that has been important for centuries. Inside are remarkable paintings and religious images, carefully preserved through many generations. Because this is a sacred site, we'll be respectful about shoes, clothing and photographs. It's a very different experience from the open-air palace landscape of Sigiriya."

### 3. Matale Kovil

"Here in Matale we can see a living Hindu tradition expressed through colorful architecture and temple worship. Sri Lanka has many religious communities, and visiting a temple respectfully offers a glimpse of that cultural richness. If there are ceremonies taking place, we'll keep a little distance and follow the guidance of the people here."

### 4. Ramboda Hanuman Temple

"As we travel higher into the mountains, the Ramayana stories become part of the landscape. In local traditions, the Ramboda area is linked to Hanuman's search for Sita. The temple is a place where that story is remembered through devotion today. The connection is part of religious tradition, which is different from the archaeological history of a monument like Sigiriya."

### 5. Nuwara Eliya

"We've left the warmer lowlands for Sri Lanka's central highlands. The air is cooler, and tea gardens cover many of the slopes. This area also brings us close to Seetha Eliya, a particularly meaningful place for people interested in the Ramayana. Tomorrow we'll visit a temple connected by tradition with Sita's story."

### 6. Seetha Amman Kovil

"This is Seetha Amman Kovil, just outside Nuwara Eliya. Many devotees associate this place with Sita's time in Lanka, and the stories include features of the nearby landscape. For people following the Ramayana, it is one of the most meaningful places on our route. Today it's a living temple, so we'll appreciate both its spiritual significance and the atmosphere of the site, while treating the epic associations respectfully as tradition."

### 7. Hakgala (if included)

"The Hakgala area is often associated in local Ramayana tourism with Ashoka Vatika, the garden mentioned in the epic. The association is a matter of tradition, not a proven identification. The present-day botanical gardens have their own character, and the natural setting is beautiful in its own right."

### 8. Divurumpola (if included)

"Divurumpola is connected by local tradition to the story of Sita's trial by fire. The different versions of the Ramayana have been told across generations and regions, so I'll introduce the association as a cultural story. It's important to respect the religious significance it may have for visitors and local communities."

### 9. Ravana Cave

"This cave near Ella carries one of the most direct local connections to Ravana's legend. According to tradition, caves in this area feature in stories about Ravana and Sita. The cave itself is a real natural place, but the connection to a specific epic event isn't historically confirmed. If conditions are safe and everyone is comfortable with the climb, we can see the cave; otherwise the story is just as interesting from our safe viewpoint."

### 10. Ravana Falls

"This is Ravana Falls, a striking waterfall on the road descending from Ella. It's named for Ravana, and local traditions connect the area with the Ramayana. It's one of those places where a landscape and a story seem inseparable. We'll enjoy the view from a safe place — the wet rocks and flowing water are not worth risking a closer photo."

### 11. Rumassala

"Even here on the south coast, the Ramayana tradition continues. Rumassala is connected in local stories with Hanuman and the mountain of healing herbs. The hill and the surrounding coast are real places we can enjoy, while the healing-mountain connection belongs to epic tradition. It's a lovely last chapter to the story trail we've followed through the highlands."

### 12. Galle Fort

"Galle Fort is a historic fortified town shaped by centuries of maritime trade and colonial history. Today it's still a living place, with streets, buildings and ocean views inside old defenses. It's a wonderful contrast to the mountain temples and waterfalls we've just visited."

## Guest-friendly FAQs

**Why do so many places in Sri Lanka mention Ravana?**  
Different communities remember and interpret the Ramayana through stories, temples, landscape names and pilgrimage traditions. This journey visits some places associated with those traditions.

**Is Ravana's story proven history?**  
The Ramayana is an influential epic and religious/literary tradition. Its exact events and associations with modern tourist sites cannot be presented as established archaeology.

**Where is Seetha Amman Kovil?**  
Near Nuwara Eliya, in Seetha Eliya. It's on the highland part of our trip, not the south coast.

**Will we visit Ravana Cave?**  
We hope to if access, weather and walking conditions are suitable. The trail can be steep and uneven, so it's conditional rather than guaranteed.

**Will there be time for Galle Fort?**  
Possibly, if the travel day from Ella goes smoothly. Rumassala and a relaxed Unawatuna arrival take priority for this trip.

**Are the times on the website exact?**  
Only when marked as confirmed by the host. Road conditions, weather and visitor access can change.

## Travel tips copy

**Temple etiquette**  
"Sri Lanka's shrines are living places of worship. Dress respectfully, follow shoe-removal instructions, ask before photographing rituals, and give worshippers space."

**Highland weather**  
"The mountains can feel much cooler than the coast. Bring a light layer, comfortable shoes and something for unexpected rain."

**Waterfall safety**  
"Enjoy Ravana Falls from a safe viewing point. Wet rocks and changing water flow can be dangerous, even when a scene looks calm."

**Coastal safety**  
"Check today's swimming flags, currents and lifeguard advice before entering the water. A beautiful beach isn't always a safe swimming beach."

**Long travel day**  
"Today includes a long scenic transfer. We'll keep the plan flexible and take breaks as needed."

## Sinhala greeting and courtesy language

Use checked and culturally appropriate pronunciation/transliteration before publishing audio:

- **Ayubowan** — traditional greeting expressing a wish for long life; commonly used as a welcome.
- **Istuti / Stuti** — thank you (transliteration varies).
- **Karunakara** — please / kindly (usage depends on context).

Avoid teaching a long phrasebook without an actual bilingual review. The app's English content should remain complete without spoken Sinhala.
